Cryptography and AI Security Lab

Collaborate with the lab

We work with researchers, companies and public-sector organisations facing difficult security and privacy problems in AI, data collaboration, payments, blockchain and digital infrastructure.

Start a conversation

A useful first conversation

Begin with the problem, not a preselected technique

Tell us what must remain private or secure, who may be malicious or untrusted, what the current system can do, and what evidence or outcome you need. That creates a much better starting point than asking for a particular cryptographic primitive in isolation.

Engagement routes

Ways we can work together

The exact form depends on the question, evidence needed and stage of the system.

01

Joint research

Collaborative research projects, co-designed questions, academic partnerships and externally funded programmes.

02

Protocol and system review

Threat-model analysis, review of privacy or security claims, design critique and independent technical evaluation.

03

Prototypes and demonstrators

Feasibility work that connects formal guarantees to measurable performance and implementation constraints.

04

Doctoral collaboration

Sponsored PhD research, industrial case studies, placements and domain input for doctoral projects.

05

Briefings and knowledge exchange

Research seminars, technical workshops, executive briefings and focused discussions for specialist teams.

06

Strategic research network

Connections across cryptography, privacy-enhancing technologies, secure AI and digital finance research.

Your first message

Four to six sentences are enough

  1. Describe the use case or system.
  2. Name the sensitive assets or security objective.
  3. Identify the relevant threat actors or trust limits.
  4. State the current stage and desired timescale.
  5. Say what outcome would be useful.

Good fit

Problems we are well placed to examine

  • Private or verifiable AI inference and collaboration
  • Federated analytics across sensitive datasets
  • Secure payment, fraud and dispute-resolution protocols
  • Oblivious transfer and private set intersection
  • Time-delayed and verifiable computation
  • Post-quantum protocols for constrained systems
